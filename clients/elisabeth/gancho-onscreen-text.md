# Elisabeth — Gancho reel: on-screen text

Reel: Elisabeth practising the padel gancho. English, no emojis, max ~7 words per line.
Target confirmed by client: **the fence** (la reja / la grille). Not "the glass", not "between the two players".

## Technique check (what the text is allowed to say)

| Point | What the sources say |
|---|---|
| When | A lob goes over the non-dominant (backhand) shoulder and a bandeja or smash isn't possible; used to keep the net. Mostly played from the right (drive) side. |
| Body position | Not a full turn: sources describe either a side-on jump (Padel Magazine) or a semi-open stance (padellog). The text avoids naming one. |
| Contact point | High, above the head, over the non-dominant shoulder, with the arm extended and the racket head pointing up. |
| Arm and wrist | The racket travels in an arc behind and over the head. Padel Magazine: "the arm gives the speed, the wrist is the last link and finds the angle." padellog wants a firm wrist, with power from the legs and trunk. Both agree the arm drives the shot and the wrist only finishes it. |
| Speed | Slow to medium, flat or lightly topspun, finishing downward. Too much speed makes the ball bounce up and hands the opponents an easy ball. |
| Target | A short cross-court angle onto the side fence (reja / malla / parrilla), so the ball takes an awkward bounce. Then get straight back to the net. |

## Main sequence (4 lines)

1. Lob over your backhand shoulder?
2. Straight arm, contact high overhead.
3. Slow and flat, finish down.
4. Gancho cross-court onto the fence.

## Alternative A

1. No room for a bandeja? Gancho.
2. Straight arm, reach above your head.
3. Arm gives speed, wrist finds angle.
4. Keep it slow. Hit the fence.

## Alternative B

1. Lobbed at the net? Keep it.
2. Ball high over the backhand shoulder.
3. Controlled pace, flat, finishing downward.
4. Short gancho angle into the fence.

## Hooks (first second)

1. Not the glass. The fence.
2. The lob that should beat her.
3. Lobbed over the backhand? Watch this.
